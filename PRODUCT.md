# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The system serves four campus complaint workflow roles. Students are the primary users because they submit complaints, track status, add follow-up information, and judge whether a resolution is acceptable. Student affairs officers are the second priority because they review, classify, assign, request information, close cases, and handle follow-up review. Department staff use the system as a daily case queue for assigned work. Administrators configure users, departments, categories, SLA settings, audit logs, reports, and email delivery.

Users are usually focused and task-oriented. Students may be uncertain or frustrated when filing a complaint, while staff need a calm operations surface that makes risk, ownership, and next action visible without decorative noise.

## Product Purpose

This is a course-project prototype of a Student Complaint and Feedback Management System. It provides local account login, role-based workspaces, case submission, anonymous or public tracking, evidence upload, department assignment, SLA signals, notifications, reporting, audit logs, settings, and profile/password management.

Success means a demo user can understand their role immediately, complete the main workflow without guidance, and trust that the interface represents an organized case-management platform rather than a generic template.

## Brand Personality

Editorial, restrained, professional.

The interface should feel warm and human, with the considered pacing from `DESIGN.md`: cream canvas, coral primary actions, dark product surfaces, generous spacing, clear hierarchy, and a calm institutional voice. It should still behave like serious operations software, so product clarity wins over decorative brand moments.

## Anti-references

Do not make the product look like a cheap admin template, a blue-and-white government portal, a flashy marketing landing page, or a purple-blue AI dashboard. Avoid generic card grids, excessive gradients, neon glow, toy-like motion, clipped half-width tables, repeated dashboard fragments, and decorative UI that competes with case work.

## Design Principles

1. Make the next action obvious. Every role should see what needs attention first: submit, review, assign, process, resolve, configure, or report.
2. Use editorial warmth without losing operational trust. The visual system may follow `DESIGN.md`, but tables, forms, filters, and settings must remain predictable.
3. Let case data breathe. Use wide workspaces, aligned tables, clear status tags, and selective dark product panels instead of cramped cards.
4. Treat risk as a first-class signal. Overdue, urgent, anonymous, unresolved, and follow-up states should be visible at a glance.
5. Cover real states. Loading, empty, error, success, disabled, focus, hover, and mobile layouts should feel intentionally designed.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast and keyboard-accessible controls. Use visible focus states, clear form labels, readable text sizes, adequate touch targets, non-color-only status meaning, reduced-motion safe transitions, and responsive layouts for desktop, tablet, and mobile.
